My research lies at the intersection of **mathematical optimization, distributed algorithms, and wireless communication systems**. I am interested in developing theoretically grounded and resource-efficient algorithms for networked systems, with a particular emphasis on understanding how communication constraints, network structures, and computational resources influence algorithmic performance.

My research covers the following four interconnected areas.

---

## Decentralized Optimization

Decentralized optimization enables multiple agents to collaboratively solve optimization problems through local computation and information exchange, without relying on a central coordinator.

My research focuses on the **design, convergence analysis, and resource optimization of decentralized algorithms**, particularly under limited communication and computational budgets. I am interested in understanding the tradeoffs between communication, local computation, and optimization accuracy, and in designing resource allocation strategies that improve algorithmic efficiency.

Recent work includes the development and analysis of multi-consensus decentralized gradient methods, as well as the joint optimization of communication and sampling resources in stochastic decentralized optimization.

---

## Wireless Communications

Wireless communications has been an important application area throughout my research. I am interested in applying mathematical optimization and algorithmic design to improve the efficiency and performance of wireless communication systems.

During my doctoral research, I investigated **intelligent reflecting surface (IRS)-assisted wireless communications**, combining theoretical analysis, optimization-based design, and experimental evaluation. While the theoretical analysis was developed under SISO communication models, the experimental investigations also included MIMO communication scenarios.

My experience with laboratory and field experiments motivates my continued interest in connecting mathematical models and optimization methods with practical wireless communication systems.

A dedicated project page will present the experimental setups, measurement activities, and results from my doctoral research.

---

## Distributed Consensus and Network Design

Distributed consensus is a fundamental building block for decentralized computation and networked systems. Its performance depends strongly on network connectivity, communication topology, and information exchange mechanisms.

My research investigates **efficient consensus algorithms and communication network design**, with particular attention to relay-assisted communication, topology optimization, and mixing matrix design.

I am also interested in relaxing conventional constraints on communication and mixing strategies to explore more flexible, resource-efficient approaches to distributed consensus.

---

## Decentralized Machine Learning

Decentralized machine learning extends distributed optimization to large-scale learning problems where data and computation are distributed across multiple agents.

My research focuses on developing and analyzing **scalable decentralized learning algorithms**, particularly those involving matrix optimization, momentum-based methods, and gradient tracking.

Recent work includes decentralized variants of the Muon optimizer, with theoretical convergence analysis and numerical investigations in matrix factorization and neural network training.

---

Across these research directions, my broader goal is to develop efficient distributed algorithms that combine mathematical rigor with an understanding of the communication and computational constraints arising in practical systems.
